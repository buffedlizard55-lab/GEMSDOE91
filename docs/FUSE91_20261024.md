**Why it won (mechanism).** (i) The metric's TPw is a *max* over
predictions within 300 m of each truth pixel, so a second dot covering
the same truth pixel earns ≈0 while still paying α·(1−k)=0.2·(1−k) of
FPw: thinning a thick surface to ~2.8 px spaced dots removes almost
pure cost (solid→dotted kept ≈85 % of geometric credit at 36 % of the
mass, GEMSDOE25 receipt). (ii) Dots within 200 m of a mapped fault
spend most of their kernel on non-test catalogue pixels; nested-pair
inversion (MODEL, assumptions in the forensics JSON) of two nested
CLAIM pairs independently implies those dots carried ≈0 hidden credit,
hidden |G| ≈ 14.1k/15.2k px (H28 fit: 12,691), TPw ≈ 5.2–5.5k,
credit/dot ≈ 0.139–0.145 vs the break-even bar α·DTI = 0.0556.
(iii) The prune kept 2,171 flank dots at 2–3 px — splay/stepover range.
(iv) The ancestor detector itself was mainly topographic: R0 dots vs
background enrich most in `det_elev_slope` (AUC 0.661) and low `tc`
(0.630 symmetric), then `tmi_hg` (0.578) (RECEIPT).

**What beating it takes (MODEL arithmetic).** At 37,654 dots and
|G|≈14k: DTI 0.32 needs credit/dot ≈0.160 (+15 % over R0), 0.4413 needs
≈0.220 (+58 %). Emission geometry on the same surface is nearly
exhausted (fleet sweep evidence); the ranking's credit density must

(HGB-CTX) to 0.2545 (+17.4 %) — the right direction and magnitude to
challenge the CLAIM band, but holdout truth (catalogue-like faults) is
not hidden truth, and the SGMC off-catalogue control disagrees
(0.0706 vs R0 0.0954, REPORT ONLY). **Beating 0.2778 is plausible, not
established; only an organizer receipt settles it.**
