Why did GEMSDOE32's 0.2778 beat its siblings — and can a unique file beat it?

Why (mechanism, proven from bytes in S22/S25 and consistent with today's diagnostics). The metric is DTI = Tw / (Tw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw + ε) with a 300 m triangular kernel (official description): each truth pixel credits only the best nearby prediction, while every predicted dot pays FP weight for the share of its kernel covering no truth. All nine leaders share one H19-5-derived ridge network; R0's edge is emission economics: dotting that network at ~2.8 px spacing, then deleting dots within 1–2 px of the mapped catalogue (6,436 dots that were nearly pure FP cost, because mapped pixels are masked from scoring while kernels keep covering the same nearby truth). Dot count anti-correlates with claimed score across the family; the pure-FP-deletion model reproduces the family's score ladder (0.1922 → 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778) within ~0.001. R0 also sits where independent methods agree: 67.67% of its dots are in ≥3-anchor consensus zones (the single strongest live-truth concentration signal in the inversion, θ ≈ +2.97), at a median 19.6 px from the catalogue.

Can a unique file beat it? In principle, yes; on today's evidence, not yet. S27 tested the strongest available lever honestly: keep S25's independently-validated detector (the only recent feature family to beat its matched holdout control with CI excluding zero) and fix its one measured defect — dot geography. Stratified emission moved median distance-to-catalogue from 5.8 px to 21.5 px with exact preregistered band shares (5/15/25/25/30%), lifted the live-anchored projection from 0.0792 to 0.1873 and the far-field SGMC proxy by +55% relative — yet both still trail R0. The residual gap is information, not geometry: only 35.75% of S27's dots sit in ≥3-anchor consensus zones vs R0's 67.67%; forced far-field dots are ranked by a surface trained on catalogue labels, so its far-field ordering is extrapolation. A >0.2778 unique file therefore needs either a detector whose far-field ranking is trained/validated specifically on isolated (catalogue-distant) faults, or genuinely new information — above all the 1 m USGS 3DEP DEM lane (free/public-domain at usgs.gov/3dep, not fetchable from this sandbox's network allowlist). Anyone claiming a present-day projection above 0.2778 for a unique raster on this 100 m stack should be required to show the same five receipts as this page.

Method in one paragraph & what was ruled out

Detector (frozen from S25, rebuilt bit-reproducibly — canary repeats exactly): oriented morphological granulometry of detrended elevation/slope (B12/B19) + gravity↔magnetics cross-spectral phase lag (B13/B14) + 23 base band/gradient features → spatially cross-fitted histogram gradient boosting on whole-component buffered quadrants. Emission (new in S27, preregistered): greedy surface-descending placement at 3.05 px spacing with distance-to-catalogue band quotas (5/15/25/25/30% over (3,5], (5,10], (10,20], (20,40], (40,∞) px) and ≤65% within-3-px caps against 26 frozen references (the caps turned out non-binding: stratification alone delivered the uniqueness). Ruled out this session: that emission geometry was the only thing separating the fabric detector from R0-level live performance — it was necessary, not sufficient. Non-fault mimics that can produce the same signatures: unfaulted dipping lithologic contacts, remanent-magnetization offsets, fan-lobe/shoreline fabrics, flight-line seams. A fault prediction is not evidence of a geothermal vent or reservoir.
