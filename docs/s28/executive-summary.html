1 · The answer in one paragraph

What scored highest among the nine sites. GEMSDOE32's gemsdoe32-h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros.tif is reported at 0.2778. It is not an organizer-confirmed score: no DrivenData receipt exists in any of the nine repositories, so every number in this table is labelled USER-REPORTED. What is verifiable is what the bytes do, and I re-derived that from each pinned file this session (SHA-256 checked against the pin before measuring): the winners emit a sparse binary dot field of ~37.6k–44k cells, spaced ≈3 px apart, sitting far from the mapped-fault catalogue (median 19.65 px = 1965 m for the top file, 69.8% of dots beyond 1 km).

Why that wins. Under the published distance-weighted Tversky index (α = 0.2 false-positive weight, β = 0.8 false-negative weight, 300 m triangular kernel) a dot that lands on an already-mapped fault earns no truth credit — the scored truth is the withheld, unmapped fault set — while it still costs 0.2 false-positive mass. Dots are therefore only valuable where they can touch unmapped truth, and piled-up dots within 3 px of a mapped trace are close to pure cost. Solving the metric algebra for the winner's reported value gives an implied weighted true-positive mass of ≈5,469 px, i.e. ≈36.0% recall of an assumed 15,195-px hidden truth set, achieved with 37,654 dots: roughly one useful dot in seven.

Can we beat 0.2778? Yes in principle — the algebra ceiling at this dot budget is ≈0.772, so there is a large gap between 0.2778 and what a perfect detector could reach. The lever is not geometry (already solved by the winners) but ranking: which cells the dots are placed on. That is what this session attacks.

3 · What this session actually built

H-S28-1 (CPT-19). A catalogue-prior transfer classifier trained on the official 19-band numeric-feature raster gems-geodawn-numerical-features.tif (sha256 4371c82e…). 41 catalogue-free layers (robust-standardised band, 400 m regional context of each band, plus a horizontal-gradient energy, a detrended-elevation curvature, and a buried-basin × conductivity product) are fed to a HistGradientBoosting classifier fitted to mapped-fault pixels vs background ≥800 m away. The probability surface is then emitted as a 37,654-dot binary field: minimum 3-cell Chebyshev separation, zero mass within 300 m of the mapped catalogue, and distance-band quotas fixed before scoring so the geometry matches the leaderboard envelope (median 20.01 px, 82.0% >1 km, median NN 3.16 px).

This is the first lane in this repository to use the official feature stack. Every previous lane — and every one of the nine sites — built its surface from hand-written single- or dual-band ridge/scarp formulas over derived products, because the official 418 MB stack had never been restored into a workspace. Restoring it took three SHA-256-verified files and one 70-second feature build.

Holdout result (shared instrument, identical emission for every arm)

Whole-component hide-and-recover, 20 % of catalogue components withheld per quadrant with a 300 m feature buffer, pixel-exact masking of the visible catalogue, pooled distance-weighted Tversky. Withheld positives n = 11,321 across four folds.

6 · Ranked hypotheses (new, untested beyond H1)

  1. H1 · CPT-19 catalogue-prior transfer (implemented above). Layers: all 19 official bands. Signature: multivariate fault expression (HG edges + curvature + strain + basin depth), learned rather than hand-weighted. Why it should find an unmapped fault: it generalises the combination of signatures rather than a single ridge rule, so a fault whose magnetic edge is muted can still be caught by gravity-HG plus curvature. Different from the repo: no previous lane used the official stack or a supervised transfer model. Cost: low (done).
  2. H2 · Multi-scale structure-tensor coherence. Layers: det_elev, det_elev_slope, tc. Signature: orientation coherence and anisotropy of topographic gradient at 300 m and 1 km. Why: under Holocene fan wash the scarp relief vanishes but the direction of the residual gradient stays coherent along strike, which curvature alone does not capture. Different from the repo: existing lanes used curvature magnitude, never orientation coherence or its cross-scale consistency. Cost: low.
  3. H3 · Vertical-gradient dip-reversal pairing. Layers: tmi_vg, iso_grav_anom_vg. Signature: coincident sign reversal of both vertical gradients across a lineament. Why: a buried normal fault offsets the density/magnetisation interface, so the vertical gradient flips polarity across the fault plane even when the surface trace is offset down-dip by Δx = z·cot(dip) under basin fill. Different from the repo: previous lanes snapped to HG crests or projected up-dip from depth_to_base_surf; none tested the polarity-pairing signature. Cost: medium.
  4. H4 · Data-driven relay/step-over completion. Layers: tmi_hg, iso_grav_anom_hg, det_elev_slope. Signature: orientation and length statistics of the catalogue, transferred to unlinked en-échelon lineaments. Why: unmapped splays are usually the relay between two mapped segments. Different from the repo: S33's tip-stepover used a fixed r=30 geometric rule; this learns the orientation/length distribution from the catalogue itself. Cost: medium.
  5. H5 · Geothermal-conduit conditioning. Layers: cond_surf, depth_to_base_surf, geod_2ndinv, geod_shearrate. Signature: high conductivity + deep basin + active strain coincident with a lineament. Why: the prize is a geothermal prize, so the withheld truth may be enriched where fluid pathways exist. Different from the repo: GDR-well priors used an external point catalogue; this uses the in-raster physics. Cost: low, but it is the most speculative — the truth may be bias-free.

The first lane after this one should be H2 (cheapest, orthogonal signature) combined with the fleet-consensus feature described in the review, which is the only variable in the 112-anchor table that predicts live score with real power.

9 · Limitations that could invalidate the projection

  • The scored hidden truth is unpublished. No local instrument can measure hidden-fault discovery; every holdout number in this project is a catalogue-recovery proxy.
  • Transfer from catalogue-recovery to hidden-fault discovery assumes the hidden faults resemble the mapped ones in the 19-band signature space. If they are preferentially in a different tectonic setting (e.g. deep basin fill with no surface expression) the classifier's ranking will not transfer.
  • The 112-anchor score model is fitted to USER-REPORTED values and explains LOSO R² 0.70 with three features; its residual spread (±0.084) is comparable to the entire gap between the best and the median site.
  • One weekly slot is at stake. If a second opinion is desired before spending it, the cheapest falsification test is H2 on the same instrument (≈10 minutes of compute).
