Why 0.2778 (GEMSDOE32) is the family's highest — and can a unique file beat it?

What the bytes prove (S22 lineage analysis, re-verified): R0 is not a better detector; it is the best-pruned member of one family. H19-5's 121,131-px ridge network (0.1922) was dotted at 2.8 px spacing (44,090 dots, 0.2600), then dots within ≤1 px of the catalogue were deleted (40,199, 0.2708 — the identical pixels on GEMSDOE28 and GEMSDOE31), then dots at (1,2] px (37,654, 0.2778). A one-parameter model that treats the deleted dots as pure false positives predicts the next two family scores within 0.001, implying R0 recovers ≈38–42% of scored truth at ≈0.135 mean credit per dot. Mechanism: mapped-fault pixels are masked out of scoring (official forum), so dots hugging mapped traces are almost pure false-positive weight under DTI = T/(0.2T + 0.2FP + 0.8|G|); claims across the nine rise as dot count falls (Spearman ρ(dots, claim) = -0.983 over nine non-independent files, recomputed today). Full derivation: R0_ANALYSIS_S22.md.

Can we generate a unique file above 0.2778? Not yet, on labelled evidence. This session's candidate is the first in several sessions to beat a strong matched control in paired holdout, and its SGMC diagnostic (0.079042) reaches 83% of R0's (0.095386); but its dots hug the catalogue (median 5.8 px; 45.90% at 3–5 px vs R0's 9.20%), so the live-anchored model projects only 0.0792. The measured fix for the next preregistered lane is emission geometry, not more features: keep the validated FABRIC surface, emit with a wider catalogue collar / distance-stratified quotas and ≤70% competitive-overlap caps, and arbitrate with SGMC->500 m plus LFI (built without fitting to LFI, to keep the projection honest). The only identified route materially past ≈0.28–0.32 remains new information — 1 m LiDAR derivatives (USGS 3DEP, free) on an unrestricted machine; this sandbox cannot reach those hosts. Method in one paragraph

FABRIC uses only the SHA-pinned 19-band GeoDAWN stack. Two new feature families target faults the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue missed because they are buried or scarp-free: (i) oriented granulometry contrast — grey-opening pattern spectra (lengths 5/11 px, 4 orientations) of detrended elevation (B12/B19), compared ±7 px across local slope breaks, so a change of fan/bedrock fabric across a trace scores highly; (ii) cross-spectral phase lag — tile-wise phase-correlation lag between the isostatic-gravity (B13) and magnetic (B14) boundaries, which a fault offsetting density vs magnetization contacts produces and a vertical unfaulted contact does not. Together with the 19 bands + 4 gradient magnitudes they feed a spatially cross-fitted gradient-boosted model (v3 whole-component buffered folds); dots are emitted by the shared NMS emitter at 3.05 px spacing/collar. Named mimics: unfaulted dipping contacts, remanent-magnetization offsets, fan-lobe/shoreline fabrics, survey seams. NoData sentinels are excluded from every statistic (masked convolution; supports eroded beyond each feature's influence radius). A fault-likelihood map is not evidence of a geothermal vent, temperature, or permeability.
