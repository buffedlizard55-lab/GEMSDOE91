Why GEMSDOE32 may have the strongest reported result

The verified masks show that the 44,090-dot D2.8 reference became a 40,199-dot H27 subset by deleting 3,891 dots within one pixel of the catalogue, then the 37,654-dot R0 subset by deleting a further 2,545 dots between one and two pixels away. No R0 dots were added. Its 0.2778 is a user-reported, unverified claim, not an organizer receipt.

Under the official 300 m triangular-kernel metric, spreading predictions can retain truth coverage while removing redundant false-positive mass; removing low-credit near-catalogue predictions may also help. This is a plausible explanation, not proof of the hidden score's cause. DrivenData confirms the known-fault mask is pixel-exact and that newly labelled faults can be nearby, so broad collars can also remove real discoveries.

The public leaderboard showed rank 1 at 0.4544 on 27 October 2026. That official-board observation does not identify a score for this project's TIFFs. Beating 0.2778 is possible in principle, but no reviewed candidate demonstrates it; no defensible leaderboard estimate is available.

Exactly how a future approved submission is entered

Do not upload the S22 file. The download above is a negative research deliverable, not an approval to upload that file. These steps apply only to a future file that passes every gate and receives separate selector authorization.

  1. Run python generate_submission.py --with-data to verify the current downloadable bytes. A format PASS alone is not permission to submit.
  2. Require complete registry clearance, fold-honest improvement over a matched incumbent, and a current quota/selector record. The official rules specify up to three submissions per week; current remaining allowance must come from the authenticated submission page.
  3. Download the approved .tif, or its ZIP containing exactly one TIFF. Do not upload this report or a JSON card.
