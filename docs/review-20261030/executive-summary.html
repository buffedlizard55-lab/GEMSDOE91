Evidence-based explanation; no score reproduction

Why is the claimed 0.2778 highest among these sites?

The best-supported explanation in the repository's earlier SHA-pinned byte-lineage report is pruning within one shared raster family, not a demonstrated new fault detector. That older audit has not been re-run here because its original source TIFFs are missing locally.

What the prior lineage record says

  • D2.8 (`GEMSDOE25`) has 44,090 dots and a `0.2600` USER-REPORTED claim.
  • H27-4 (`GEMSDOE28/31`) is recorded as 40,199 dots after removing 3,891 dots within 1 pixel of the known catalogue; the shared-file `0.2708` value is USER-REPORTED.
  • R0 (`GEMSDOE32`) is recorded as 37,654 dots after removing a further 2,545 dots 1–2 pixels from the catalogue; `0.2778` is USER-REPORTED.
  • GEMSDOE36 is recorded as R0 plus 1,200 dots with a `0.2750` USER-REPORTED claim.

The arithmetic is consistent with selective deletion of 6,436 near-catalogue dots raising the reported value while most of the support remains. It does not prove those pixels were false positives.

Why pruning can raise a fault score

The official metric uses a distance-weighted Tversky index with α=0.2, β=0.8, and 300 m triangular distance support. False positives contribute to the denominator. If a removed dot carries little credit against a hidden new fault, removing it can reduce false-positive mass while preserving much of the matched credit.

That mechanism is conditional: a new fault may lie near a known trace, and the 300 m kernel means proximity alone is not enough to classify a dot as wrong. The score claim is unauthenticated; this review cannot infer the hidden truth or reproduce the public score.

Could it be beaten? Possibly, but no defensible numeric forecast exists here. A fresh, unique candidate has not been generated, the assigned lane and data are unavailable, and the reported public score does not validate transfer to private or expert-revised evaluation. The answer is not estimable, not a projection.
