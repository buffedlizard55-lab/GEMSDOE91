## Why these are plausible — and what they do not establish

- The organizer identifies the task as mapping geological faults indicative of geothermal resources; the public fault inventory is explicitly incomplete and may contain inaccuracies. The private test contains expert-identified faults outside the public catalogue, and final scoring may use an expanded label set. Thus, reproducing catalogued lines is not discovery.
- The official USGS GeoDAWN description distinguishes magnetic measurements relevant to subsurface structure/geology from radiometric measurements relevant to surface geology/soil and notes coordinated lidar acquisition over a similar extent. This supports testing independent, physically distinct evidence rather than blending correlated bands without controls.
- The USGS Great Basin structural-discontinuity summary and 2026 slip/dilation report support stepovers, terminations, and stress-compatible structures as mechanisms. They do **not** show that any particular raster pixel is a fault or that a feature will improve competition DTI.
- The 2026 GeMS/SGMC release is a promising official context source, but its mapped units vary in scale and are not reconciled across state borders. An apparent high-resolution boundary is not uniformly reliable ground truth.
- The official competition metric has asymmetric false-positive/false-negative weights and a 300 m distance kernel. This motivates careful coverage-versus-clutter experiments, but does not justify a leaderboard estimate or a threshold chosen from public claims.
