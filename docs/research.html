The uniqueness conflict is mathematical.

The candidate’s surface had maximum measured absolute Spearman 0.038236; 89 indexed entries were attempted, with missing entries marked UNVERIFIED. Final-dot testing stopped at reference 27 on 100% overlap; 62 entries were deliberately not checked afterward. This is not universal uniqueness.

Local geometry audit, not newly verified score measurements.

DTI = T / [0.2T + 0.2(S − M) + 0.8|G|]

T is best triangular-kernel credit summed over truth; S is emitted mass; M is prediction-weighted match to truth. T and M are not interchangeable. A dot may cover multiple truth pixels.

Removing unmatched or redundant low-credit mass may reduce false-positive cost while retaining truth coverage. It does not follow that every redundant dot costs exactly 0.2, that thinner always wins, or that this explains the entire reported score. The needed causal evidence—organizer truth or controlled organizer receipts—is unavailable.

Could a distinct method score higher? In principle, yes, by earning more new kernel credit per unmatched mass. This test does not demonstrate it. The historical website’s ZIP was also malformed: 216 bytes containing an empty ZIP member, not a TIFF. Our new ZIP’s member and exact TIFF bytes are automatically checked.
