A plausible metric mechanism is sparse-emission economics. Under the official distance-weighted Tversky definition, α=0.2, β=0.8, and the 300 m triangular kernel, the repository's equivalent expression is `DTI = T / [0.2T + 0.2(S−M) + 0.8|G|]`, where `T` is weighted truth credit, `S` is emitted probability mass, `M` is matched mass, and `G` is truth. Deleting a dot can reduce penalized unmatched mass while leaving much of `T` intact if other nearby dots already saturate the max-kernel credit. Conversely, deleting a unique truth-covering dot lowers `T` and can hurt because false negatives carry β=0.8. The geometry change therefore offers a plausible explanation, **not a verified causal decomposition or a recipe**. Hidden labels plus a receipt, or a valid same-label ablation, would be needed to establish why an organizer score changed. The catalogue hide-and-recover holdout is structurally unable to establish the private-test effect of removing catalogue-near dots; prior PRUNE holdout results were zero after the collar, as documented in S18.
