## What probably drove the 0.2778 claim—and what can be inferred

Repository-maintained forensics describe a nested lineage: a dense H19-5 ridge surface was thinned to roughly 2.8-pixel spacing, then progressively pruned around mapped catalogue faults. This is consistent with a metric effect: under a distance-tolerant Tversky objective, nearby dots can compete for overlapping truth-side credit while each still contributes prediction-side penalty; pruning dots near known catalogue faults can remove predictions that are excluded from truth evaluation or unlikely to match an *unmapped* fault. A sparser pattern can therefore raise precision-like utility without establishing a better geological detector.

This is a **plausible mechanism, not a causal finding**. The reported score has no verified receipt-to-raster binding in the pinned manifest, the variants are nested rather than independent trials, and no blinded reproduction against the competition's private labels is possible. It does not show that the dots identify geothermal vents: fault prediction is at most a structural-prospectivity proxy for permeability, not direct evidence of a vent, heat flow, or fluid discharge. Could a genuinely new method score above 0.2778? **In principle, yes; no available evidence here supports claiming that it will, or estimating how much higher.**
