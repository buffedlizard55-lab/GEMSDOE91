Why h33-h33-2-b2 got the highest supplied score — PhD analysis, then how to beat it

  1. Ridge surface: The h19-5 multi-physics ridge network (121k px continuous) was the fleet's best localizer. Sparse emission matters because DTI pools max kernel credit per truth cell (capped at 1) but pays 0.2·FPw for excess mass. Thinning dense ribbon → 44k dots at 2.8-px spacing already yields +0.068 (0.1922→0.2600) without a new detector.
  2. Collar prune: Hidden truth is fault pixels absent from existing_faults.tif. Dots at d≤2 px of mapped faults earn ~0 hidden credit (visible faults are masked pixel-exactly) but still pay FPw. Deleting 3,891 dots (→0.2708) then 2,545 more (→0.2778) removes 0-credit mass — metric-geometry, not geothermal proof.
  3. Can we beat it? Not by re-thinning the same surface: its implied weighted recall ≈37% caps rearrangements at +0.01–0.025. Beating 0.2778 needs a better localizer of blind structures (75% of Basin systems are blind, Faulds & Hinz). LFI shows truth density is learnable (LOSO Spearman 0.957, projected 0.38–0.41 at same budget). Contrarian bet: target blind faults under cover (CBSC cond×depth) or strain jogs, not scarps. This session's CBSC-E1 tested cond×depth concordance and failed (0.0000 HOLDOUT), proving the simple gradient product is insufficient — negative is a deliverable.
