## What explains the reported 0.2778?

The mathematical mechanism is plausible but the premise is unverified. Maximum kernel-weighted credit rewards an extra prediction only where it improves existing credit; redundant predictions may add false-positive mass. Sparse emission can therefore improve DTI without better geological localization. Conversely, two predictions less than 300 m apart can improve different truth pixels, or the second can be closer to the same truth pixel. A 300 m separation is **not** a theorem of optimal emission. A fixed far-field quota is likewise not demonstrated optimal.

The GEMSDOE32 repository page was freshly read through GitHub in this session and explicitly says `UNSCORED`, `NO ORGANISER SCORE EXISTS`, and calls its nearby 0.2747 a model/projection. Thus **0.2778 is USER-REPORTED / UNVERIFIED, not ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED**. The prompt's 0.3195 and 0.4413 leaderboard claims also have no fresh organizer receipt in this session. No new leaderboard score or improvement is claimed. Different candidate surfaces and emission budgets confound the historical cross-site comparison. A controlled, same-split density ablation and a genuine submission receipt would be needed for a stronger conclusion.
