What was tested

HCPA fused a conductivity-to-basement-depth proxy, basement-step concordance, thermal-field information, and extensional kinematic context to seek concealed hydrothermal fault continuations. The test withheld whole fault segments with a buffer, used only visible-fault catalogue features, masked visible faults exactly, and computed pooled DTI with the 300 m triangular kernel (`alpha=0.2`, `beta=0.8`).

That mechanism remains scientifically plausible but not unique: saline/clay basin fill, basin-margin contacts, sparse thermal sampling, broad regional strain, roads/drainage, and calibration-grid coverage can produce similar spatial signals. The implementation did not localize withheld faults well enough to clear the project's quality bar.

Why sparse dot geometry did not save it

DTI gives max triangular-kernel credit around truth pixels and penalizes excess emitted mass. Sparse spacing can reduce redundant cost, but it cannot make a poorly ranked surface discover faults. The 30,000 / 37,654 / 44,000-dot E3 diagnostics scored 0.073231 / 0.084995 / 0.094359 HOLDOUT-DTI, respectively; none approaches 0.267782. The frozen 37,654-dot geometry is also a directed spatial duplicate.
