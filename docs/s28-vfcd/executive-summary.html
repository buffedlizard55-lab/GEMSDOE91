The identity that explains everything

Official metric (DrivenData problem page, pooled): DTI = T / (0.2·T + 0.2·FP + 0.8·|G|). Known-fault pixels are masked out of scoring (DrivenData staff, forum thread 11516), and a "new fault" is by definition any pixel not in the catalogue (forum 11536). Therefore a dot 1–2 px from a mapped trace is almost pure false-positive cost: it can neither touch a masked pixel nor a "new" fault by definition. Deleting near-catalogue dots raises the score without moving any surviving dot.

R0 is not a better detector — it is the best-pruned member of one family. Its own dot geometry: median dot 19.65 px from the catalogue, ~13.5% average credit per dot, roughly 9 of 10 dots unmatched (MODEL bracketed by the unobservable matched mass).

Can we beat it?

  • With a unique raster (the standing requirement): not with the current evidence. Every independent de-novo lane in this repo projects 0.08–0.19 MODEL; the family occupies the zones where the truth-density model says value lives (×19 where three independent fleet rasters agree). Our S28-VFCD experiment confirms this from a new angle: vent-field information improved neither the holdout nor the projections.
  • With a derivative of the family (further pruning): possible in principle (oracle pruning of 5k/10k dead dots would give 0.294/0.313 MODEL) but unidentifiable — the GEMSDOE36 +1,200-dot experiment shows far-field "model" dots carry ~1/10 of R0's average credit — and it is excluded by the uniqueness requirement.
  • With new information: the identified escape. A public rank-5 team states it uses "20 m lidar-derivative U-Net ensembles" (forum 11531) — resolution and supervision we cannot obtain in this sandbox (USGS 3DEP 1 m lidar is free and official, but the hosts are unreachable here; labels.tif truth labels for supervised training are not pinned anywhere in the mirrors).
  • Instrument warning (design around it): the hide-and-recover holdout measures catalogue recovery and correlates only +0.105 with the live score across 112 anchors — R0 itself scores 0.0067 on it (collar paradox). That is why this page leads estimates with the live-anchored model and always labels both.

Bottom line. 0.2778 = "0.2600 minus every dot that could only lose money", executed exactly. Beating it needs either dead-dot identification (unidentifiable from public data) or a new data layer (unavailable here). The honest high-score recommendation stands: the best-scoring known submission is GEMSDOE32's file — already submitted, do not resubmit; the unique submissions available are expected to score lower.
