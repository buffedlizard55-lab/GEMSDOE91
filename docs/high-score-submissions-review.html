What can be said about GEMSDOE32’s 0.2778—and what cannot

The analysis below uses the repository’s recorded pixel/lineage forensics and the score sequence supplied in the prompt. It supports a plausible mechanism; it cannot establish that those reported decimals came from the competition organizer.

  1. Candidate surface: prior forensic notes describe a catalogue-blind ridge network built from finer-scale topographic/geophysical context. A better localizer can raise the share of predictions near hidden faults, but public files alone do not reveal the private target labels.
  2. Sparse emission: reported lineage goes from 121,131 ridge pixels to 44,090 dots under 2.8-pixel spacing, while the listed value rises from 0.1922 to 0.2600. The DTI rewards distance-tolerant coverage and penalizes excess predicted mass, so thinning redundant predictions is mathematically plausible as a gain mechanism.
  3. Catalogue-collar pruning: lineage notes report 3,891 additional dots removed near the catalogue (listed 0.2600→0.2708), then 2,545 more removed (listed 0.2708→0.2778), producing a 37,654-dot raster. Since the target is faults absent from the public catalogue, spending mass directly on known traces may have low value. This is a metric-consistent interpretation, not proof that the pruning caused an organizer score.
  4. Limits: the historical numbers are not controlled holdout results; the stages change emission and pruning, and their scores are unverified. Do not infer leaderboard generalization, a causal effect size, or geothermal-vent detection from this chain alone.

Detailed internal forensic records: ISO91 review, DENOVO historical report, GEMSDOE32 byte/geometry record, and prior three-pass repo review. These are project records, not organizer receipts.
