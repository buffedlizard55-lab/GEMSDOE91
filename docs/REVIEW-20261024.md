## What the GEMSDOE32 evidence actually says

[Source snapshot](https://github.com/buffedlizard55-lab/GEMSDOE32/blob/078f3cea1bc75d15beb81b095ab488a912327a5d/docs/index.html)
was retrieved using GitHub's contents API on this date. It describes deletion of
near-catalogue dots from the ancestor and states: **“NO ORGANISER SCORE EXISTS
for this or any artifact in this repository.”** It also explicitly calls its
projected live DTI a model, not a score. The prompt's reported performance is
therefore not ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED in this review.

Mechanistic explanation, not a measured result: removing predictions that add
false-positive mass without useful hidden-truth kernel credit can improve a
precision/recall tradeoff. Sparse dots can cover fault corridors with less mass
than solid swaths under a distance kernel. But deleting near known faults could
also remove useful continuations. Hidden-truth geometry and a genuine receipt
are needed to distinguish these explanations. More pruning is not automatically
better. No defensible forecast of beating the reported benchmark follows from
this evidence.
